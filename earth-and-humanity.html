Guide

Different frameworks.
One shared timeline.

A band represents a span of time. A marker represents an event. Their positions on the timeline allow you to see what happened concurrently.

How do I use sliders and hide bands?

The position slider moves the visible window within a wider selected range. The second slider changes the window’s span, from 10 years to the entire timeline. The “Show / hide tracks and events” panel lets you toggle whole tracks, organism groups, regions and individual organisms or events. Search by name or filter the list; “Hide matches” hides all filtered results. “Show all” restores the full chart. Each view retains a linear time axis; only the slider’s choice of window width is logarithmic.

Do organisms form a simple chain of ancestors?

No. Overlapping sub-bands show ranges known from the fossil record, not certain dates when all populations originated or became extinct. Side branches of evolution are included. Hominids (Hominidae) include humans and other great apes; hominins belong to the human lineage after its split from the chimpanzee lineage. A “first” fossil is the oldest described evidence, not necessarily the true origin of the group.

Where are climate and economic changes shown?

Regional tracks beneath the periods include separate rows of environmental and economic markers. A circle marks a natural phenomenon, a square marks human environmental impacts or interactions, and a diamond marks economic change. One event may concern several regions. Descriptions distinguish observations, models and hypotheses about causation. Earliest cultivation, domestication and later widespread use are not the same date.

How is geological time divided?

Eon → era → period → epoch → age. Their chronostratigraphic counterparts are eonothem → erathem → system → series → stage. Subepochs and subperiods are also shown, as is the informal Precambrian supereon. Not every level is formally subdivided in every interval.

Are the Chipped Stone Age and Paleolithic separate periods?

The “Chipped Stone Age” is a traditional name for the Paleolithic, while the “Polished Stone Age” refers to the Neolithic. The Mesolithic is a transitional stage in some regions. The Neolithic also encompasses the spread of farming and sedentary settlements; stone-working techniques alone do not define its beginning everywhere.

Why do human-history periods overlap?

Agriculture and metallurgy developed at different times in different regions. That is why the Levant and Western Asia, the lands of present-day Poland, and European history have separate tracks. Dates are approximate and based on regional conventions, and historical period boundaries can have alternative turning points.

What exactly defines a boundary?

A GSSP is an agreed point in a specific geological section, often identified through a fossil, chemical change or climate record. Older units may use numerically defined GSSA boundaries. The age in millions of years is an estimate of a boundary’s age, not necessarily its formal definition. The detail view distinguishes these cases.

How should I interpret dates and very short intervals?

Ma means million years and ka means thousand years. b2k measures years before 2000 CE, while BP measures years before 1950 CE. This timeline stores dates relative to 2000; the calendar has no year zero. Ancient events have approximate dates. Every view uses a linear scale, so human history almost disappears on a timeline covering billions of years: choose a closer range. Short bands retain their actual width; their names can be found in the catalog.
